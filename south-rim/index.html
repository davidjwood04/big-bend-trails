Trail Details

About This Trail

The South Rim Loop is the grandest hike in Big Bend National Park — a full traverse of the southern Chisos rim with views that extend 200 miles into Mexico on clear days. The trail climbs from the Basin through either the Pinnacles or Laguna Meadow route to reach the rim at roughly 7,200–7,400 feet, then follows the exposed edge for nearly two miles before looping back. At 12–14 miles depending on your chosen route, it is the longest and most committing day hike the park offers.

The payoff justifies every step. Standing on the South Rim, you're looking straight down a 2,000-foot cliff face into the Chihuahuan Desert of northern Mexico. The Sierra del Carmen glows rust and amber to the east. The Fronteriza range fills the southern horizon. On good days you can see volcanic peaks 150 miles away. There is no comparable vantage point in Texas.

For Scouts, this is the capstone hike of the trip — saved for the group's strongest day and strongest legs. It is longer than Emory Peak and covers more exposed terrain. The recommended loop goes up via the Pinnacles Trail and returns via Laguna Meadow, allowing you to ascend on a more direct route and descend through the shaded upper drainage.

⚠ Plan this one carefully. At 12–14 miles with 2,100 ft of gain at high elevation, the South Rim requires the same preparation as Emory Peak — early start, full water, real food, designated sweep adults. Do not attempt this without a prior warm-up hike. It is not a hike to do on Day 1.
Pacing Guide — Pinnacles Up / Laguna Meadow Down
  • Mile 0 Chisos Basin Trailhead (5,401 ft) — Same trailhead as Emory Peak, behind the lodge. Depart by 6:00 AM. Water and restrooms at the Basin store. Fill every container — water is unavailable until you return to the Basin. Trail begins on the Pinnacles Trail heading south and climbing immediately.
  • Mile 2.8 Pinnacles Pass (~6,800 ft) — The trail crests a high saddle with dramatic views of the Basin below and Casa Grande peak above. This is a natural rest point roughly a third of the way in. Check in on the group here — anyone flagging should consider whether to continue. The rim is still 2+ miles of climbing ahead.
  • Mile 4.5 Emory Peak Junction — Trail intersects the Emory Peak spur. Continue straight on the South Rim Trail. From here the trail traverses high terrain toward the rim proper, passing through open pinyon-juniper forest with increasingly wide views opening to the south.
  • Mile 5.5 South Rim — First Overlook (~7,200 ft) — The rim edge arrives suddenly. The desert drops away 2,000 feet below your feet into Mexico. Stop, rest, take it in. This is the moment the whole hike builds toward. The maintained trail continues east along the rim for another 1.5 miles of continuous cliff-edge views.
  • Mile 7.0 Southeast Rim / Boot Canyon Junction (~7,400 ft) — The trail reaches its highest and most exposed point, near the Boot Canyon junction. The Sierra del Carmen range fills the eastern horizon. This is the turnaround for the rim traverse — the return route heads north via Laguna Meadow. Rest here for 20–30 minutes before the descent.
  • Mile 7–12 Laguna Meadow Descent back to Basin — The return via Laguna Meadow is gentler in grade than the Pinnacles ascent, descending through open grassland and oak-pine forest. The meadow itself (around mile 9) is a beautiful flat stretch — use it to regroup and redistribute snacks before the final push back to the Basin. Return trailhead approximately mile 12–14 depending on exact route taken.

Troop Logistics

The South Rim is the same 6:00 AM departure as Emory Peak — it takes the same amount of time or more. Get to the rim by 10–11 AM to have time on the edge before the afternoon builds. The rim is fully exposed with no shelter; lightning at 7,400 feet is a life-safety issue. Be off the rim by 1:00 PM regardless of conditions.

This hike requires 3 liters of water per person minimum. The distance and duration are unforgiving if anyone runs dry. Pack a full lunch — the rim is one of the best picnic spots on earth. Bring warm layers; the rim sits 2,000 feet above the Basin and wind is constant and cold even in October.

The recommended route is Pinnacles up, Laguna Meadow down — this puts the steeper, more direct climb in the morning when legs are fresh, and the gentler descent in the afternoon. The reverse is possible but harder. Both routes start and end at the same Basin trailhead, making this a true loop.

Note: if the group plans an overnight, the South Rim has designated backcountry campsites that require a permit from the NPS backcountry office at Panther Junction. As a day hike, no permit is needed.

⏰ 6 AM start. Rim by 10–11 AM. Off exposed rim by 1 PM. Same discipline as Emory Peak — non-negotiable.
🌩️ The rim is fully exposed at 7,400 ft. Lightning in October is rare but real. No metal poles, no hesitation to retreat.
🧥 Bring a wind layer. The rim is cold and constantly breezy even when it's warm in the Basin below.
🍱 Pack a real lunch for the rim. You'll be out 7–9 hours and the South Rim is the best picnic spot in Texas.
🗺️ Download the NPS Big Bend offline map before leaving. Junctions are signed but the loop covers a lot of ground.
🏕️ This is the best hike to save for the end of the trip — after the group has their trail legs and knows the park.

The Hardest Part Comes First

The Pinnacles route front-loads the difficulty — 1,400 ft of gain in the first 2.8 miles at sustained 10% grade. Once you crest Pinnacles Pass, the trail eases considerably. Get the hard climbing done early while legs are fresh, and the reward of the rim walk feels earned.

The Rim Walk Is Effortless

The 1.5-mile rim traverse is nearly flat — it exists purely for the views. Walk it slowly. Stop often. The South Rim is one of the places in this park where you should put your phone away and just look. The descent via Laguna Meadow is gradual and forgiving compared to the ascent.

Summit Timing — Oct 9–13

Depart the Basin trailhead by 6:00 AM. Arrive the South Rim first overlook by 10:00–10:30 AM. Complete the rim traverse and begin the Laguna Meadow descent by 1:00 PM at the latest — afternoon storms are the primary hazard at exposed rim elevation in October. Back at the Basin by 3:00–4:00 PM. Plan a hot dinner and early bedtime; this is the hardest day of the trip. The Basin store closes at 6 PM — shop before you hike if you need supplies for dinner.
