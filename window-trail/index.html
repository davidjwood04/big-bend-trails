Trail Details

About This Trail

The Window Trail is the Chisos Basin's signature hike and one of the most deceptive trails in the park. At 5.6 miles round-trip with only 800 feet of elevation change, it looks manageable on paper — but the catch is the direction. You go downhill the entire way out, descending through Oak Creek drainage toward the iconic V-shaped pour-off that frames the western desert 1,000 feet below. Then you turn around and climb all 800 feet back to the Basin.

The trail begins behind the Chisos Basin store and campground, immediately entering the shade of a lush riparian corridor — Texas madrone, bigtooth maple, and Arizona cypress line the creek drainage, making this one of the greenest, most protected walks in the park. In October the maples show color. The further you descend, the more the canyon walls press in around you.

The pour-off at trail's end is spectacular: a smooth-polished granite shelf where Oak Creek drops off the edge of the Basin rim into open air. You can sit on the rock and stare out at the Chihuahuan Desert floor a thousand feet below. It's a natural picture frame — hence "The Window." The return climb is where the trail earns its moderate rating — steady and relentless over 2.8 miles. Budget more time for the return than the outbound leg.

Pacing Guide
  • Mile 0 Chisos Basin Trailhead (5,401 ft) — Start at the trailhead behind the Chisos Basin store, near the amphitheater. Restrooms and water at the store. This is the last water until you return — fill up completely. The trail shares the first 100 yards with the Window View Trail before splitting; take the lower fork.
  • Mile 0.3 Junction with Laguna Meadow Trail — Stay straight (downhill) on The Window Trail. The descent into Oak Creek drainage begins in earnest here. Shade from the canyon walls and riparian trees makes this one of the coolest stretches of trail in the park. In October look for bigtooth maple color change on the slopes.
  • Mile 1.0 Oak Creek crossing area — The trail crosses and re-crosses the seasonal creek several times through here. In October it's typically dry or a trickle. Watch footing on creek-bed rocks — algae can make them slick even when barely wet. The canyon narrows noticeably from here.
  • Mile 2.2 Lower canyon narrows — The walls close in and the trail becomes rockier underfoot. The drainage floor is polished granite in sections. Pace slows here for most groups. This is the last good spot to turn around for anyone who's unsure about the return climb — the pour-off is 0.6 miles further.
  • Mile 2.8 The Window Pour-off (~4,620 ft) — The trail ends at the granite shelf pour-off where Oak Creek drops into open desert. The V-shaped gap frames the Chihuahuan Desert and distant mountains. Sit, eat, drink water, and rest before the return. Do not approach the edge — the rock slopes steeply and the drop is fatal. Allow 20 minutes here. Return the same way — uphill all 2.8 miles.

Troop Logistics

The Window is the most accessible hike from the Basin — the trailhead is a 2-minute walk from the lodge and campground, no driving required. That makes it the default evening or recovery-day hike for the group. Leave around 3:00 PM to hit the pour-off at golden hour and catch the last light framing through The Window. You'll be back at the Basin by nightfall.

The return climb is where inexperienced hikers struggle. Plan for the outbound leg to take about 1 hour, and the return 1.5–2 hours with a group. Reserve water for the climb back — it's easy to drink too much on the easy downhill and have nothing left when you need it most. Each Scout needs at least 2 liters.

One genuine hazard: the Oak Creek drainage is a flash flood corridor. If you see or hear weather building over the mountains, turn around immediately and move out of the drainage. October thunderstorms are uncommon but possible. Check the NPS forecast before heading out and don't enter the lower canyon narrows if storms are anywhere in the vicinity.

The Deceptive Trail

Going down is easy — the 2.8-mile outbound leg feels like a pleasant stroll. The return is where it reveals itself. 800 feet of uphill over 2.8 miles at 5,000 ft elevation, after you're already tired. Plan for the return to take 30–40% longer than the outbound leg with a group.

Best Basin Hike for Shade

Unlike Lost Mine or Emory Peak, most of The Window Trail runs through shaded canyon drainage with tree cover. In October this means comfortable hiking even in the afternoon. The lower narrows stay cool all day — a real advantage for an after-lunch or late-day departure.

Recommended order: Lost Mine (Day 1, acclimate to altitude), Emory Peak (Day 2–3, full summit day), The Window (afternoon/evening hike any day — starts and ends at the Basin), Santa Elena (afternoon add-on, requires drive).

Best Time to Hike · Oct 9–13

Depart the trailhead at 3:00 PM for the optimal experience. You'll arrive at the pour-off around 4:00–4:30 PM — right as the afternoon sun drops toward the desert horizon and turns The Window's frame golden. The return climb in the cooling late afternoon is far more pleasant than midday heat. You'll be back at the Basin by 6:30–7:00 PM, in time for dinner at the lodge or camp cooking. Bring a headlamp just in case — October sunset is around 7:20 PM and the canyon gets dark early.
